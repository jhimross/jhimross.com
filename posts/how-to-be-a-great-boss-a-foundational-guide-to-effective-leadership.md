## **Defining the "Boss"**

![](https://jhimross.wordpress.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/05/pexels-photo-684387.jpeg)Photo by Lukas Blazek on [Pexels.com](https://www.pexels.com/photo/close-up-photo-of-a-person-wearing-suit-jacket-684387/)

In modern workplace culture, the word "boss" is sometimes used with hesitation, replaced by trendier, politically correct titles like "coach" or "team leader." However, these terms are not all-encompassing. The title of boss is one of responsibility and accountability. It originates from the Dutch word **baas** , which was historically a term of respect used to address a person in charge.

> **Definition:**  A boss is "someone in charge, who leads and manages people."

Choosing to be a "boss" means accepting the responsibility to create an environment where people show up because they _want_  to be there, not because they _have_  to. Never use the phrase "I’m the boss" in an overbearing or entitled manner; instead, wear the title with pride. While anyone can hold a title, the true measure of a boss is the engagement and performance of the workforce they lead. Internal makeup is the engine of leadership, but the impact is measured by the engagement of those who follow.

Moving from a disengaged to an engaged workforce requires a boss with a specific internal makeup. Internal makeup is the engine; the LMA formula is the steering wheel. Without the former, you have no power; without the latter, you have no direction.

## **Get It, Want It, and Capacity (GWC)**

![](https://jhimross.wordpress.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/05/pexels-photo-6147363.jpeg)Photo by Diva Plavalaguna on [Pexels.com](https://www.pexels.com/photo/people-holding-gray-puzzle-pieces-6147363/)

To be effective, a boss must pass the "GWC" test. Unlike general traits, these serve as a binary filter. For a boss—or any employee—to be in the "Right Seat," the answer to all three must be an **emphatic YES**.

  * **"Get It":** This is natural aptitude or "biochemistry." It is the intuitive feel for the job, the pace, and the systems.
  * **"Want It":** This is the "fire in the belly." It is a genuine, unforced desire to lead that cannot be taught.
  * **Capacity:** This is the actual ability to perform the work, measured by four markers:
    1. **Emotional Capacity:** Being self-aware enough to know how you are influencing people, remaining humbly confident, and connecting authentically.
    2. **Intellectual Capacity:** The brains to predict, plan, and solve complex problems. As F. Scott Fitzgerald noted, this is "the ability to hold two opposed ideas in the mind at the same time, and still retain the ability to function."
    3. **Physical Capacity:** The stamina and energy to finish what you start and get your hands dirty when needed.
    4. **Time Capacity:** The self-discipline to organize your schedule to focus on leading rather than reacting.



**The GWC Contrast:**  A "not-so-good" boss views employees as a burden, fearing that training them is a waste of resources. In contrast, a **Great Boss** , such as Trevor Moses of imageOne, understands that developing people is a core responsibility. By increasing their skills, you make them more valuable, and better people invariably create a better company. Having these internal qualities is only half the battle; they must be applied to the team through strategic placement.

## **Surrounding Yourself with Great People: The "Right Person, Right Seat" Strategy**

![](https://jhimross.wordpress.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/05/pexels-photo-7698813.jpeg)Photo by Yan Krukau on [Pexels.com](https://www.pexels.com/photo/coworkers-tossing-papers-at-an-office-7698813/)

"Great People" are defined by the intersection of two components:

  1. **Right People:** Those who share your company's **Core Values** (the soul of the organization).
  2. **Right Seat:** Those who have the **GWC** (aptitude, desire, and capacity) for their specific role.



A boss must be relentless in addressing the **Four People Issues** , as each drains the boss's time and energy:

  * **Right Person, Right Seat:** The goal. These individuals need encouragement, not micromanagement.
  * **Right Person, Wrong Seat:** They fit the culture but struggle with the role. This drains time because the boss must constantly re-assign or coach someone who lacks the GWC for their current seat.
  * **Wrong Person, Right Seat:** High performers who don't share values. They are "toxic" and drain time by undermining the culture, causing "thump-thump" (miscommunication) and friction.
  * **Wrong Person, Wrong Seat:** These individuals drain the most time through constant error correction and conflict.



If a boss ignores these issues, the primary risk is that the "Dianes" (the Great People) will feel discouraged and leave for leaders who provide a healthier environment. Once the right team is in place, the boss must master the LMA formula to maintain engagement.

## **The LMA Formula: Leadership + Management = Accountability**

![](https://jhimross.wordpress.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/05/pexels-photo-5324975.jpeg)Photo by Anna Shvets on [Pexels.com](https://www.pexels.com/photo/colleagues-discussing-details-of-project-on-meeting-5324975/)

Accountability is not a demand; it is a byproduct of the equation **L + M = A**.

Leadership (Working **ON**  the Business)| Management (Working **IN**  the Business)  
---|---  
**Vision:**  Thinking, Direction, and Creating the Opening.| **Traction:**  Doing, Expectations, and Communication.  
  
Growth is the result of combining these two. As Thomas Edison stated, "Vision without execution is hallucination." In this framework, **Vision + Traction = Growth.**  Without both, an organization becomes either a "nightmare" (no vision or traction) or "rudderless" (traction without direction).

## **The 5 Leadership Practices**

![](https://jhimross.wordpress.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/05/pexels-photo-207896.jpeg)Photo by Pixabay on [Pexels.com](https://www.pexels.com/photo/silhouette-of-people-during-golden-hour-207896/)

Great leaders provide "Vision" by mastering these five habits:

  1. **Giving Clear Direction:** Sharing a compelling vision that answers the "8 Questions" regarding the company's destination.
  2. **Providing Tools:** Ensuring employees have the training, technology, and your personal time.
  3. **Letting Go of the Vine:** You must stop "taking monkeys." When a direct report brings a problem (the monkey) to your office, your goal is to ensure they **walk out with their monkey.** Build autonomy by asking, "What would you do?"
  4. **Acting with the Greater Good:** Making decisions based on the company's long-term reputation (the "third 10" in the 10-10-10 rule) rather than personal ego.
  5. **Taking Clarity Breaks:** Scheduled time away from the office to think. Use a blank legal pad and ask:
     * What is the one ‘people move’ I must make this quarter?
     * What seems overly complicated that must be simplified?
     * Am I focusing on the right things?
     * Do I have the Right People in the Right Seats?
     * What can I delegate to be more effective?



Leading provides the "why," but management provides the "how."

## **The 5 Management Practices**

![](https://jhimross.wordpress.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/05/pexels-photo-7793692.jpeg)Photo by Yan Krukau on [Pexels.com](https://www.pexels.com/photo/colleagues-having-a-team-building-7793692/)

To gain "Traction," a great boss implements these five practices:

  1. **Keeping Expectations Clear:** Synchronize with direct reports in four specific areas:
     * **Roles:** The major responsibilities of the seat.
     * **Core Values:** Behavioral expectations.
     * **Rocks:** The 1-7 priorities for the next 90 days.
     * **Measurables:** Using **leading indicators** to predict success. Andrew Park (P.B. Industries) transformed safety not by tracking "accidents" (a trailing indicator), but by measuring "hard braking" and "speeding" (leading indicators).
  2. **Communicating Well:** Maintain an **80/20 ratio** where the direct report does 80% of the talking. Use **Echoing** ("What I hear you saying is...") to eliminate "thump-thump" and ensure synchronization.
  3. **Meeting Pulse:** A weekly cadence of meetings that start and end on time with a focused agenda.
  4. **Quarterly Conversations:** A dedicated, informal time every 90 days to review GWC and Core Values.
  5. **Rewarding and Recognizing:** Highlighting those who meet measurables and live the values.



## **Conclusion**

![](https://jhimross.wordpress.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/05/pexels-photo-6804091.jpeg)Photo by cottonbro studio on [Pexels.com](https://www.pexels.com/photo/project-manager-planning-tasks-6804091/)

Being a great boss is a choice that requires the discipline to **Delegate and Elevate**. Many bosses fail to let go because of "personal head trash"—the internal excuses that "no one can do it as well as I can" or "it's faster if I just do it." This creates a bottleneck and pushes you to 120% capacity, which is a recipe for burnout.

To avoid this, you must clear your "head trash," trust your Great People, and elevate yourself to the tasks you love and are great at.
